# Independent numerical evaluation

The standard-library references in `evals/numeric.py` use transfer functions and specification equations, not generated DSP, product filter coefficients or the product's crossover reference. `evals/gates.json` freezes tolerances, case inventory and stress fixtures. Changes require dedicated review with retained baseline evidence.

EQ is the product of bilinear transforms of analog shelf and peaking transfer functions. The evaluator measures complex steady-state sinusoidal transfer at 15 points over 44.1/48/96 kHz, checks 0.2 dB magnitude error and 2.5% complex relative error. Analytic anchors independently verify center gain and shelf asymptotes. A deliberately wrong dB factor, added gain or one-sample delay must fail the actual EQ evaluator, including its phase check.

Compressor references declare the 10 ms channel-energy detector followed by attack/release exponential smoothing of desired reduction in dB. Low/high/low DC steps at three rates measure both the entire curve (0.25 dB) and the 63.2% transition crossing (10% or two samples). These include detector lag rather than incorrectly identifying total attack with a single time constant. Independent static hard/soft-knee tests and ratio-one identity supplement the curve.

Neutral multiband compression uses ratio one. Its measured complex transfer is compared against independently transformed LR4 filters and their phase-compensated sum. Flat magnitude alone cannot establish time-domain identity. Raw phase coloration is recorded explicitly.

Alias stress covers nine periodic single-tone fixtures: rates 44.1/48/96 kHz; bins 683/1093/1531 of an 8192-frame period; amplitude 0.5, drive 30 dB, tone 5 kHz. Fourier quadrature computes intended harmonics of the analytic periodic tanh input after source-rate pre-emphasis. Ideal bandlimiting retains harmonics below source Nyquist. Tone and DC transfer use 2048x/4096x internal rates; RMS convergence must be at most 0.0001 before judging the product. This avoids reusing the product's interpolation/decimation filters. A lower trial rate (256x/512x) failed this unchanged convergence gate and was increased; the gate was not weakened.

All 1x/2x/4x waveform errors are retained. Declared integer delay is checked (0 or 32 samples); one gain and fractional delay are fitted using only the fundamental, then applied to all intended harmonics. Error retains higher-harmonic, finite-filter and residual alias contributions. The 4x versus 1x improvement gate is 10 dB at every declared fixture; failures and exceptions remain in `metrics.json`/`failures.json`. This is a bounded steady-state stress sweep, not universal nonlinear quality, transient correctness, listening or hardware qualification.

Other cases check per-channel ingress clamp at all declared sample-rate boundaries (8/44.1/48/96/192 kHz), 75 accepted/rejected scalar parameter boundary decisions, block invariance, deterministic resets and known integer-delay nulls. The initial independent 8 kHz run exposed preparation validating the 5 kHz default before applying valid supplied settings; its failed command is retained in `artifacts/dsp-independent-first`. New implementation runs must demonstrate the fix; this case stays required.
